Saathiya × Kho Gayi

A Bollywood DnB remix built entirely through prompting with Claude Code. No DAW. No Python experience since 2014. Just ears and natural language. Two sittings, eight months apart.

The Original Tracks

The Long Version

2:57. Sonu Nigam opens with "teri ek hansi ke badle", Kho Gayi's drums tease in underneath and pull out so Saathiya's bassline leads into a half-time drop, where "hanste rehna" (keep laughing) lands. The antara gets room to breathe, builds back, and breaks into a 176 BPM two-step.

Part 1: February · Finding the Drop

3 sittings · ~17 hours · 464 renders · the moments that changed everything

Beat-Warped Wobble Problem
Each beat individually time-mapped. Audible speed fluctuations, like someone tapping the tempo button.
Constant Stretch Breakthrough
5 AM. One constant time-stretch instead of beat-by-beat warping, the way DJ software does it. No wobble. The foundation.
The Snap Breakthrough
The drop always felt late. Cutting every Saathiya stem to zero on the downbeat and bringing Kho Gayi in at full volume fixed it.
4-Bar Flute Loop
Two bars felt like a skipping record. Four bars kept the whole phrase, and the phrase is the hook.
Bass Distortion, Medium
Tanh saturation on the mids, clean sub underneath. The sweet spot between too clean and too aggressive.
Zero Drum Restarts Feb Final
A 4-bar drum loop restarted every 11 seconds. Kho Gayi's continuous drop section removed the start-stop feel, and showed its real tempo was 95.0, not 95.7.

Part 2: October · Same Ears, Newer Model

1 sitting · 9 listening rounds · 27 full-length renders · 4 new scripts

Re-measuring everything
The 1:17 length was a constraint, not a choice: the flute had to loop because there seemed to be only 10 seconds of it. Re-measuring both songs with a newer Claude model showed Saathiya is locked at exactly 88.000 BPM for all five minutes, in a key one step from Kho Gayi's (B major next to E major). So the whole song could play at native speed with no stretching and no looping. Kho Gayi also had a second drop that was never used.
The First Long Version Breakthrough
Round 1. Kho Gayi's own second build under Saathiya's flute interludes, into its second drop. It came out in the middle of the track and sounded like the opening the song was missing.
Real DnB Tempo Breakthrough
At 88 BPM one bar is two bars at 176, so the flute already fits drum and bass tempo. A two-step built from Kho Gayi's own kick and snare, with its hi-hats on top. "v3 is gnarlyyy"
Reading the Lyrics Breakthrough
Whisper transcribed the vocal stem with a timestamp for every word, which turned the song into phrases that land on the beat. "Hanste rehna" means "keep laughing". Getting this cut right took three tries: the first two started inside the hum at the end of "madham", and only ears caught it.
Nine rounds of listening
Every round was the same loop as February: listen, describe, render. The notes that shaped the final arrangement:
  • "The hook starts mid lyric" → every vocal section now starts at the top of a line
  • "Build 2 sounds like a great beginning", then "ek hansi starting feels so unique" → Sonu opens the track
  • "No warning it changes into d&b" → Kho Gayi's drums tease in on the second "Saathiya" and pause for the bassline
  • "Kho Gayi abruptly stops for Sonu" → the guitar fades out under his first two bars
  • "Is it worth it to slow down for him?" → yes, but the antara builds back into the drop "build-back is sickkk"
The Long Version Final
Round 9. Sonu opens, half-time drop A, the antara, then the 176 drop into the fade.
